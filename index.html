The idea

A small monitoring device is fitted to each illuminated sign, either at the workshop when the sign is made or on site for signs already mounted. It reports over the mobile network whether the sign is lit. When a sign fails, the monitoring platform raises an alert and creates a repair job for the technicians, who go and fix it, often before the retailer notices.

Sign fails→ Device detects it→ Platform raises an alert→ Repair job sent to technicians→ Sign fixed and confirmed working

Sold as a monthly subscription per sign. Scope for now: illuminated signs only, not other in-store equipment.

Why it matters to the sign maker

  • Find failures before the client complains, which helps meet maintenance-contract response times.
  • Fewer wasted trips: technicians know which sign failed, when, and how (fully dark or partly dark).
  • A new recurring revenue line on every sign sold, and a reason for clients to choose us over other sign makers.

Overall

The model works elsewhere: in Germany, Lichtwart sells exactly this through sign makers, at €399 hardware plus €8 a month, about €13.40 per sign per month over five years. We found no one in the Baltics selling monitoring for static illuminated signs yet. The closest local player, impressBox (Vilnius), monitors digital screens and is testing LED fault detection from power use (beta), the same method we would use. Ready-made certified devices and cheap mobile connectivity mean a pilot fits within about €10k. Lithuania alone is a small market, so the long-term business is Baltic-wide.

TopicFinding
DevicesReady-made devices exist. They cost about €15 for Wi-Fi smart relays, about €60–150 for LoRaWAN plus a gateway, and about €80–210 for devices with their own mobile connection. The most reliable way to detect failure is to measure the sign's power with an in-line meter, which also catches partial failures. Many clip-on clamps can't read the low current of an LED sign (under 1–2 A). A meter wired after the sign's timer can't tell "switched off on schedule" from "broken", so the device must handle that.
ConnectivityUse a device with LTE-M, with NB-IoT and 2G as fallback. In Lithuania:
  • Telia: NB-IoT since 2019/2020 and LTE-M on the 900 MHz band freed by its 3G switch-off (Dec 2022). It also has the strongest network overall (5G reaches 99.7% of households, per the regulator RRT), keeps 2G until at least 2030, offers a free 1-month IoT trial and an IoT management platform, and already builds partner monitoring solutions. That makes Telia the most likely connectivity and sales partner. It publishes no LTE-M coverage figure.
  • Bitė: the only operator that publicly claims LTE-M covers all of Lithuania (since Feb 2023). It sells NB-IoT and offers a 6-month trial pack of 10 NB-IoT SIMs.
  • Tele2: no public LTE-M or NB-IoT launch in Lithuania, but Tele2 IoT (Sweden) lists both on its network and offers a 2-month trial kit.
3G is off (Telia Dec 2022, Tele2 Mar 2025, Bitė Dec 2025). 4G covers 97.9% of the territory (RRT). Operators don't publish IoT prices; reseller plans cost about €1.6–2.5 a month excl. VAT. 1NCE sells a 10-year IoT SIM for €12 (500 MB) with LTE-M in Lithuania, but no NB-IoT. Test both Telia and Bitė at real sign sites.
RulesFitting a device to a sign already on a building is electrical installation work up to 1000 V. It needs a VERT installation attestation, held by a company or an individual electrician, with attested staff (Energy Law Art. 22; VERT ruling O3E-1388). Fitting at the workshop is likely product assembly, to be confirmed with VERT; connecting the finished sign on site still needs an attested installer. Outdoor illuminated advertising on TN supply systems must be protected by a 30 mA residual-current breaker (Lighting Installation Rules, point 50), and normal leakage must stay at about 10 mA or less, so the device's own leakage counts. Certifying our own device is estimated at about €12–40k (an EU Commission study puts cybersecurity testing alone at €5–50k+), so the pilot uses already-certified devices under their original brand. Putting our own brand on a device would make us its legal manufacturer (RED Art. 13). Outdoor advertising still needs a municipal permit (Advertising Law Art. 12). No rule covers hidden internal parts; a visible box may breach the approved design, especially in heritage zones.
MarketFuel stations and pharmacies have the most signs, followed by grocery chains (Maxima, Iki, Rimi, Lidl, Norfa), telecom shops, banks and fast food. If every Lithuanian sign subscribed at €5 a month, revenue would be about €0.5M a year. A realistic share is lower, which is why the Baltics matter. Store counts checked in October 2026: Maxima 241, Iki 245+, Rimi 87, Lidl 87, Norfa 159; Camelia ~350, Eurovaistinė 260+, Gintarinė 250+, BENU 80+ (1,227 pharmacies in total); Viada 133, Circle K 99, Neste 85, Orlen 30. The estimate may count some signs twice where pharmacies and telecom shops sit inside malls or grocery stores.

Decision: the device has its own mobile connection

Most sites won't give access to store Wi-Fi, and chain IT departments rarely allow outside devices on their network. So the device must work without store Wi-Fi, on its own mobile connection (LTE-M / NB-IoT, with 2G as fallback). Wi-Fi devices such as Shelly are kept only as an exception, for sites where Wi-Fi or a separate router is already available.

Main risks

Willingness to pay

A dark sign costs little at a single store. The case relies on maintenance contracts and brand image across many sites. The pilot must prove it.

Small home market

Lithuania alone is roughly €0.5M a year at most. Real scale needs Latvia and Estonia, and possibly Poland.

False alarms

Timers, power cuts and partly failed LEDs must be told apart from real failures, or clients will ignore alerts.

Competition

Lichtwart could enter the Baltics. impressBox already sells screen monitoring to the same retailers and is testing LED fault detection. Telia or Civinity could add sign monitoring to what they already sell. Moving early matters.

Idėja

Prie kiekvienos šviečiančios iškabos montuojamas nedidelis stebėsenos įrenginys: dirbtuvėse gaminant naują iškabą arba vietoje, jei iškaba jau sumontuota. Jis mobiliuoju ryšiu praneša, ar iškaba šviečia. Iškabai sugedus, stebėsenos platforma pateikia įspėjimą ir sukuria remonto užduotį technikams, kurie nuvyksta ir sutaiso iškabą – dažnai dar prieš tai, kai gedimą pastebi prekybininkas.

Iškaba sugenda→ Įrenginys tai užfiksuoja→ Platforma pateikia įspėjimą→ Remonto užduotis išsiunčiama technikams→ Iškaba sutaisyta ir patvirtinta, kad veikia

Parduodama kaip mėnesinė prenumerata už kiekvieną iškabą. Kol kas apimtis – tik šviečiančios iškabos, ne kita parduotuvės įranga.

Kodėl tai svarbu iškabų gamintojui

  • Gedimai aptinkami dar prieš klientui pasiskundžiant, todėl lengviau laikytis priežiūros sutartyse numatytų reagavimo terminų.
  • Mažiau tuščių išvykų: technikai žino, kuri iškaba sugedo, kada ir kaip (visiškai užgeso ar šviečia tik iš dalies).
  • Nauja pasikartojančių pajamų eilutė už kiekvieną parduotą iškabą ir priežastis klientams rinktis mus, o ne kitus gamintojus.

Skirtingi mokesčiai esant tam pačiam iškabų skaičiui

Mokestis už iškabą per mėnesįPajamos per mėnesįPelnas per mėnesįMūsų dalis per metusMūsų suma per visą sutartį

Kaip skaičiuojama: pelnas per mėnesį = iškabų skaičius × (mokestis − ryšys − platformos kaina) − pastovios veiklos išlaidos. Vienkartinis įrenginių antkainis = iškabų skaičius × (įrenginio kaina − savikaina). Abu dalijami pagal „mūsų dalį“; pradinės išlaidos atimamos tik iš mūsų dalies. Neįskaičiuota: montavimas (atlieka ir už jį ima iškabų gamintojas), remontas (iškabų gamintojo priežiūros sutartys), mokesčiai ir anksčiau laiko nutrauktos prenumeratos.

Pagrindinės rizikos

Pasirengimas mokėti

Vienoje parduotuvėje užgesusi iškaba kainuoja nedaug. Verslo pagrindas – priežiūros sutartys ir prekės ženklo įvaizdis daugelyje vietų. Tai turi įrodyti pilotinis projektas.

Maža vietinė rinka

Vien Lietuva – daugiausia apie 0,5 mln. € per metus. Tikram mastui reikia Latvijos ir Estijos, galbūt Lenkijos.

Klaidingi įspėjimai

Laikmačiai, elektros dingimai ir iš dalies sugedę LED turi būti atskiriami nuo tikrų gedimų, kitaip klientai ims ignoruoti įspėjimus.

Konkurencija

Lichtwart gali ateiti į Baltijos šalis. impressBox jau parduoda ekranų stebėseną tiems patiems prekybos tinklams ir bando LED gedimų aptikimą. Telia ar Civinity galėtų prie savo paslaugų pridėti iškabų stebėseną. Svarbu judėti anksti.
